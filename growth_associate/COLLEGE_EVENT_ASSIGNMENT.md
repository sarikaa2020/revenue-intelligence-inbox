# College Event Planning and Review

## Your challenge

Choose one specific event or fest connected to your college and explain how it is planned, run, and reviewed.

- **If you have helped plan or conduct an event:** use that event and describe what actually happened.
- **If you have not:** choose a real, named event or fest at your college and plan how you would run its next edition. You do not need to organize or run it for this assignment.

Choose a specific event, not a generic idea such as “a college fest.” Explain what the event is, who it is for, how it usually works, and what makes it relevant to your college. Use evidence you can access, such as an academic calendar, event page, poster, published headcount, or your own event materials. Clearly label confirmed facts, estimates, and assumptions.

## What to submit

1. **A 5–8 minute video walkthrough.** You can speak to camera, share your screen, or use both. Use the video to explain your choices from the first planning decisions through the post-event review.
2. **A short event plan** in a document, spreadsheet, or slides. Keep it practical and easy to follow. Include the event timeline, budget or resources, approval steps, event-day schedule, promotion idea, and recording plan.
3. **Optional supporting material.** You may attach photos, clips, posters, schedules, budgets, approvals, or other evidence that supports your explanation.

## What to cover in your video

### 1. The event and its audience

- Name the event and your college. If relevant, identify the edition or year.
- Explain what the event is, why it is held, and who usually attends.
- Describe the event’s scale using any reliable numbers you can find, such as student headcount, venue capacity, registrations, or attendance.
- Explain the event goal and how you would measure success. For a past event, share the actual goal and result where you know them.

### 2. Timing and pre-production

- Explain how the date and timing were chosen. Use the academic calendar, exam schedule, or other college-specific information where relevant.
- Walk through the event concept, venue, schedule, team roles, budget or resources, promotion, registration, and attendance tracking.
- Show your preparation timeline and the main risks or last-minute changes you considered.

### 3. Approvals and documentation

- Explain which approvals were needed, such as venue, budget, promotion, use of college branding, or permission to record.
- Describe how the approvals were requested and confirmed. For a proposed event, show who you would approach and when.
- Mention the documents or materials used, such as an event brief, budget, agenda, run of show, promotional content, or checklist.
- Be honest if something was informal or was not documented.

### 4. Event day

- Explain your role and what other people handled.
- For a past event, compare the plan with what happened, share attendance or other results, and describe one challenge you handled.
- For a proposed event, walk through the event-day schedule, responsibilities, and how you would respond to a likely issue.

### 5. Recording and post-production

- Explain what you would photograph or record and why. Include a simple shot list or video outline in your event plan.
- Describe how footage would be selected, edited, captioned, and prepared to share.
- Explain how you would get permission before recording or publishing identifiable people.
- For a past event, describe how the footage or photos were handled. If no recording exists, say why and what you would capture next time.

### 6. Post-event review and your contribution

- For a past event, compare results with the original goal and explain what you would repeat or change.
- For a proposed event, explain what you would measure afterward and how you would document results, costs, follow-up, and final approvals.
- Be clear about which decisions and tasks you owned and which were handled by others.

## Evidence and privacy

Supporting material is optional. Attach only items that help explain or support your account. In your video, point out what each item shows. You may redact names and sensitive details. Do not share private messages, phone numbers, attendee lists, or identifiable photos unless you have permission.

For a proposed event, do not present estimates or planned approvals as actual results. For a past event, if you no longer have an original document or recording, say so rather than recreating it as if it were original.

You may use AI tools to help prepare your materials. Your event choice, evidence, assumptions, decisions, and explanation should reflect your own understanding. Mention briefly where you used AI.

## What we assess

- How well you understand the specific event and its audience.
- Whether your plan is practical, complete, and supported by college-specific evidence.
- How clearly you explain approvals, responsibilities, risks, and measurement.
- Your judgment about what worked, what is uncertain, and what you would improve.
- How clearly you communicate your own contribution.
